Here we are: macOS 27 (Golden Gate) is out. It's been a long time since I last
reviewed new releases of an OS I've been using for quite a long time now. I
won't repeat what other reviews already pointed out. Rounded corners of varying
size are gone, as is the translucent sidebar that was added on all macOS default
apps. What else? AI assistant is not available in Europe, at least till October.
I don't use AI and so I don't really care. Today I noticed that a little
contextual writing assistant popover menu appears when you select a good portion
of text. It's Siri AI in action, although it is disabled on my system. See it by
yourself:

{{< figure src="/img/2026-09-15-21-23-06.png" >}}

I never used Siri, I didn't even tried the option offered today. Sure, I could
ask Siri, or ChatGPT, or Claude to fix all the English typos you'll find on this
personal website but I won't do that. Otherwise, it wouldn't be my personal blog
anymore. If you're okay with that, that's good.

Besides, it seems macOS 27 is less hungry in terms of memory even if Siri, which
I am not using at all (and it is also supposed to be deactivated), is running in
the background. It looks like this is a release similar to those we've seen in
the past, where Apple is consolidating the existing stack rather than
introducing new features to be beta tested world wild. For someone who had (by
choice) stuck with Mojave, who, moreover, mainly uses the command line, and who
switched back to macOS two years ago, I'm only contemplating how OS X became
what is now called macOS. Good things from the past remain, though, and I don't
feel all that lost. Of course, I'm biased since I mostly live in a terminal but
now I can use a GUI instead of TUIs for most of my daily drivers: newsboat/News
Explorer, senpai/bIRC, neomutt/Apple Mail, tmux/Apple Terminal (why not?). I
didn't find any alternative to Neovim; BBEdit is so good, but I spent too much
time with Emacs or Vim apparently, and I missed an interactive REPL and/or
modal editing.

Incidentally, I drafted the following post last month but never got to its end.

I'm always looking for native macOS app rather than, say, electron-based
applications. The only notable exception is probably [Typora][1], which I used
to use as a Markdown viewer for a long time now. Today I learned that someone
coined them [Mac-assed][2] app instead. Why do they feel so good or why do I
reach for them first? This is not about SwiftUI vs. AppKit (I do not really like
SwiftUI), or the infamous rounded corners that got an upgrade in recent versions
of macOS -- which I only notice when running Safari, Mail, Photos or Music on my
desktop.

Together with those [pesky icons][3] added to every single menu I believe these
are the most dominant complaints about Tahoe I've seen in recents months

Of course I'm biased since I have been working on MacBooks for over twenty years.
I barely know how to use a Windows machine (and I don't care, TBH), and I don't
really think this year will be the one for Linux on the Desktop once again.
There's something unique to macOS (and MacBook as well), but it has already been
[discussed elsewhere][4].

Services, Quick Actions available through Automator are builtin stuff. Now it's
time for Shortcuts, with a native command-line interface (try `shortcuts help`
in your terminal). Even if AppleScript may seem like a thing of the past, it is
handy to be able to write scripts that talk to graphical applications, and I
found myself writing or adapting existing scripts.

And what about those nifty [tips & tricks][5] that I mentioned in the past? Or
maybe those cool [drag and drop][6] actions? All those [shortcuts][7], perhaps?

Texshop has no equivalent when it comes to talk $\TeX$, whether it be plain
$\TeX$, $\LaTeX$ or CONTEXT. Apple Notes is probably one of the best note taking
app, at least it remains the cheapest among the top apps, and Reminders does
quite a good job actually. Its transparent interface with Agenda makes it a
perfect fit on the OS as a whole.
