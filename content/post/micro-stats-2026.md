I left Twitter a while ago and replaced it with the micro-posting system that
sits on the main page of this website. I kept the habits of using hash tags, not
only in those micro-posts but also in all my notes or reminders. It prove at
least to be useful in the macOS ecosystem since we now have tags available in
the Reminder app. Here's a word cloud of what pill up along the years:

{{< figure src="/img/2026-11-21-19-23-44.png" >}}

At first I was surprised that Emacs and Python were that frequent in my
postings, but then I realized that (1) I was using a different writing ecosystem
when I started the micro-post section on this site, and (2) my posting activity
has largely diminished with time.

Back in the days, I had some Emacs shortcuts to capture the current front page
on Safari (URL and page title) in an Org file or to simply paste it, formatted
as a Markdown link into a micro-post. I think this was something that was
inspired by or grabbed directly from the [Irreal][1] website. These days I tend
to accumulate links into the Reminder app, and from time to time I start writing
a bunch of micro-posts to unstack the working list. The same often happens after
reading some posts on Hacker News and by following links online, one thing
leading to another.

In total, I have 4481 Markdown files. More than half (2451) of the posts were
posted between 2018 and 2020. If I look at my posting activity in 2019, here's
what I get with Mathematica:

{{< figure src="/img/2026-11-21-20-22-32.png" >}}

As for 2026, I only have 282 posts at the time of this writing:

{{< figure src="/img/2026-11-21-20-23-52.png" >}}

I’ve never set myself any rules for writing on this site over the last twenty
years, and that’s not likely to change. I don’t think I have any less time or
motivation than I used to. So perhaps my micro-posting activities have shifted
towards actual micro-posts rather than mere reposts or link archiving.
