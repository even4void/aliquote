For the past 10 years my video/audio setup was comprised of a Denton amplifier,
a DAC, an old gen Apple TV, a turntable, a CD player that got broken in the
meantime and two hi-fi speakers of good quality. The Apple TV was used to
connect my iPhone or MacBook to the amplifier via an optical to coaxial
converter plugged onto the DAC. Likewise the sound from my TV was redirected via
an optical cable to the DAC. I also add a direct connection via a coaxial from
the CD player to the DAC but to be honest built in DAC in the CD player was
already good enough so that it makes little difference if any.

Today I'm left with a 4K Apple TV and an HomePod which means a lot less of
cables lying around here and there. To be honest I don't really care about the
video setup since I barely watch TV shows these days. I know I can still get a
decent audio output from my amplifier, but maybe a second HomePod which I could
pair with the present one would do the job as well. As for streaming my music
via Airplay, the combo Apple TV + HomePod means instant feedback. While I had to
wait for 4-5 seconds before the old Apple TV wake up and start playing, it's now
instant: Just press the play button and listen to the music. As when you open
your laptop and get prompted with your login screen. That's what Steve Job
wanted from the beginning:, after all: that the system get ready instant.

Of course, in case the network is down nothing's working anymore in the house.
That's the 21st century, right? Thankfully I will still be able to play my old
records on the turntable! Or listen to some of the 20k songs I keep archived on
my HD using headphones or AirPods.

Even if haven't tested at the time the old Apple TV was still connected to my
home network I'm pretty sure it would have been possible to stream Apple Music
to both Apple TVs so that I could get the audio playback redirected to my DAC
from the old Apple TV while the newest one would have been using the HomePod.
